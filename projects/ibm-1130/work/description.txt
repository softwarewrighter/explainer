In 1965, IBM released a computer that would teach a generation how to program. The IBM 1130 was small enough for universities, powerful enough for real science. Now you can learn its assembly language right in your browser.

In this video:
- The IBM 1130 minicomputer and its historical significance
- Architecture overview: ACC, EXT, IAR, index registers, flags
- Punch card simulator: source cards vs object deck cards
- Selectric console printer: assembler listing at 15.5 CPS
- Complete instruction set: LD, STO, A, S, SLA, SRA, BSC, BSI
- Live demo walking through progressive challenges
- Memory-mapped registers and indexed addressing

The 1130's clean architecture makes it perfect for understanding how computers really work. These concepts still apply to modern processors.

Try the emulator: https://sw-comp-history.github.io/ibm-1130-rs/
Source Code: https://github.com/sw-comp-history/ibm-1130-rs
